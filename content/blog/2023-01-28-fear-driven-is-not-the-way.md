title: 😱 Fear-driven is not the way

Fear is one of the worst motivation tools I can imagine. Some examples of fear-driven actions:

- **Writing cryptic code** and **avoiding documenting** so that the company you work for **can't replace you**.
- **Not testing enough** or **not writing tests** out of **fear of missing a deadline**.
- **Forcing yourself** to impress your wife out of **fear of losing her**.
- **Working 22 hours a day** because of **fear of missing a deadline**.
- **Not asking for a raise** out of **fear of getting fired**.
- etc.

All of these are destructive actions, and this is true both for a person doing things out of fear and for the one using fear as a motivation.

- **Writing cryptic code** and **avoiding documenting** → bus factor, long onboarding, inability to set up common standards.
- **Not testing enough** or **not writing tests** → bugs, inability to ship on time, too much time spent on manual testing.
- **Forcing yourself** to impress your wife → various personal and family problems, probably a divorce.
- **Working 22 hours a day** → low performance, lots of bugs, burnout.
- **Not asking for a raise** → leaving the company for a better salary, getting through onboarding and adaptation again.

All of the above are either self-fulfilling prophecies when the result is what one was trying to avoid, or a much longer and inefficient path to the desired outcome.

As a manager you should avoid using fear as much as possible. As a person you should always ask yourself two things:

1. Would failing what is feared really leads to imagined consequences?
2. Would the shortcut you're going to take work long-term?
