Software *frameworks*, like other products, *follow a life-cycle*: birth, adoption, stability, support, and decline. Yii is no exception.

Frameworks *evolve to adapt* to technological changes and community practices. For Yii, this has resulted in Yii2 and Yii3, each addressing PHP advancements and new standards. However, transitioning to a *new version poses challenges*, such as a *lack of early adopters and doubts about the framework's future*. The development team must remain committed during these periods.

Once *stable*, frameworks *can stay relevant through regular updates*, as seen with Yii1, Yii2, Symfony 2, and to be seen with Laravel. Yet, *fundamental changes* eventually *need total rewrite* to keep up with evolving standards. Without continuous innovation, frameworks stagnate and become obsolete.

Currently, *Yii3 is in its early adopter phase*, where feedback shapes its final form. This stage is crucial but challenging for the ones trying it due to possible issues.

Developers should **choose frameworks based on their needs** — prioritizing *stability* or opting for *newer features with potential risks*.

Understanding this lifecycle is necessary to make informed decisions and appreciate the efforts in maintaining and evolving frameworks.
