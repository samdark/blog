💎 **Big companies** usually value clean planning, global strategical goals, and clean scope a lot. It makes total sense:

1. If the company is big, the product is, likely to be big and complex as well. Even if the product is simple, the backend usually looks like the lower part of an iceberg.
2. Not fixing a non-critical issue clients are requesting quickly is OK. Even not fixing an issue at all won't kill you. User base is large, and non-critical issues aren't experienced or considered a problem by everyone.
3. Since the system is big, things to fix and improve are endless.
4. There are enough resources for both maintenance and new features. Usually, that's multiple teams doing that.

Overall, comprehensive planning and following a vision, a plan and its deadlines are top priority. Practices like iterative planning, sub-goals, and scrum sprints fit it well. Within a two-week period (usually), plans do not change unless there's an emergency.

💎 On the other hand, there are **startups**. Unless you're very lucky to raise a huge sum right away (you might think it is a good thing initially), you have to live in a harsh environment.

1. Resources are extremely constrained, i.e., there is no money, limited team hours, etc.
3. Every client is gold. You're spending your own hard-earned money. Losing a client means even less resources. Not fixing a client issue likely leads to the client leaving.
4. Things to fix are still limited and mostly come from client feedback.

Planning and global goals are still needed, but they are of lower priority. Also, deadlines are usually not met because client issues tend to eat up the majority of resources available. In these circumstances, even two-week sprints do not fit the process well. It's better to use Kanban boards with priority-effort sorting to avoid introducing unnecessary process complexity.

> Morale: Look at what's actually needed for the company / business. Do not blindly apply processes. Especially when there's a company size mismatch.
